# TrevorTrader Go-to-Market & Sales Strategy

**Status:** Working strategy, October 2026  
**Purpose:** Build an evidence-led trading software and media business.  
**Core principle:** Publish wins and losses, distinguish paper from live results, and never claim a proven edge without sufficient evidence.

## 1. Vision and positioning

TrevorTrader can become two complementary businesses:

1. **Media and education:** TrevorTrader Academy on YouTube, original trading recaps, educational videos, advertising and appropriately disclosed sponsorships.
2. **Software subscriptions:** A scanner, research and signal tools, and potentially broker-connected automated execution.

The differentiator is transparency: explain every trading decision, disclose costs and losing trades, and demonstrate performance using reconciled broker records and out-of-sample tests. The silver-and-blue TrevorTrader BOT is the recognizable brand character, not a substitute for real analysis.

**Proposed positioning:** *Trade the strategy. Without emotion.* This describes rules-based execution, not guaranteed profitability.

## 2. Customer segments and jobs to be done

- **Learners and YouTube viewers:** Understand momentum trading, risk, and how the BOT operates.
- **Self-directed active traders:** Find a manageable shortlist of momentum opportunities without scanning dozens of tickers manually.
- **Experienced systematic traders:** Evaluate signals, test exits, and review trade-level evidence.
- **Potential automation customers:** Execute a chosen, risk-limited strategy through a supported broker, subject to operational and regulatory readiness.

Research each segment's willingness to pay and actual workflows before building a broad product.

## 4. Media engine: TrevorTrader Academy

### Recurring programming

- **Daily — Life of the BOT (3–5 minutes):** What the BOT scanned, why it did or did not trade, entry/exit points, realized outcome, fees/slippage, and one lesson. No-trade days are legitimate episodes.
- **Weekly — BOT vs. Market:** Trade count, win rate, gross and net P&L, drawdown, notable mistakes, what changed, and comparison to appropriate benchmarks.
- **Evergreen Academy lessons:** Momentum, relative volume, float, news catalysts, MACD, VWAP, Level 2, first-new-high setups, risk controls and common execution mistakes.
- **Short clips:** Original, educational excerpts pointing to full analyses, not mass-produced near-identical uploads.

Use the recognizable silver-and-blue BOT head as the narrator/visual identity. Build each video from genuine decision-level logs, charts, reconciled fills and original commentary. Keep footage and data usage within vendor licensing and redistribution rights.

### Distribution funnel

**YouTube discovery → TrevorTrader.com → free evidence and educational content → email/waitlist → scanner trial or paid beta → subscriptions.**

Capture opt-in emails, offer a useful free resource, publish a clear product roadmap, and measure visitor-to-signup and signup-to-paid conversion. Avoid implying that a viewer can replicate paper results.

### Media monetization

- YouTube advertising, after meeting the platform's then-current eligibility and monetization requirements.
- Relevant, clearly disclosed sponsorships and carefully vetted affiliate partnerships.
- Educational products only where they provide distinct value and comply with applicable financial-promotion rules.
- Software subscriptions as a separate recurring revenue stream.

Avoid broker incentives or sponsors that compromise research independence. Sponsorships should never determine what trades or outcomes are reported.

## 5. Illustrative economics — not forecasts

**YouTube ads:** At an assumed $5 RPM, 1,000,000 qualifying long-form monthly views would imply approximately **$5,000/month** in creator ad revenue before expenses and taxes. Actual RPM, monetized views, geography, seasonality and Shorts economics vary considerably.

**Subscription example:** 1,000 paying subscribers at $49/month produce **$49,000 monthly recurring revenue (MRR)** before processing fees, data licenses, hosting, customer support, refunds, taxes and churn.

The scenario of 1 million monthly views converting at 0.1% into 1,000 subscribers is **illustrative only**. Video views are not unique people, and attribution, conversion, retention and price acceptance must be measured independently.

| Paid subscribers | $19/month | $49/month | $99/month |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | $1,900 MRR | $4,900 MRR | $9,900 MRR |

This table assumes all subscribers are on a single tier and excludes all costs. Track contribution margin and retention, not just gross revenue.

## 6. Launch sequence

### Phase 0 — Research integrity and working demo
- Freeze and version strategy rules; retain full logs, configuration hashes and broker-reconciled fills.
- Report entry ask and exit bid, spreads, slippage, fees, no-trade reasons, and MFE/MAE.
- Separate paper, live pilot and hypothetical/simulated outcomes prominently.
- Target roughly **100 trades per experiment** as an initial evidence milestone, not a statistical guarantee; evaluate day-level uncertainty, drawdowns and holdout/prospective performance.
- Publish an honest evidence dashboard and a clear risk disclosure.

### Phase 1 — Audience and discovery
- Publish **30 high-quality, original YouTube videos** as an initial content milestone.
- Build a free subscriber/waitlist funnel on TrevorTrader.com.
- Interview prospective users about scanner pain points, desired workflows and willingness to pay.
- Monitor returning viewers, retention, click-through rate, qualified waitlist signups and audience feedback.

### Phase 2 — Scanner beta
- Recruit **10–20 testers** for a narrowly scoped scanner and research beta.
- Start with known pilot traders, then recruit outside the immediate network.
- Test reliability, timeliness, false positives, user engagement, support burden and willingness to pay.
- Consider a limited paid beta only after users demonstrate recurring value.

### Phase 3 — Subscription launch
- Release scanner and research tiers with clear terms, data latency disclosures, billing and cancellation.
- Use educational YouTube demonstrations, public evidence, referrals and targeted partnerships.
- Measure activation, weekly active use, free-to-paid conversion, churn, support cost, acquisition cost and gross margin.
- Expand only after retention and unit economics justify it.

### Phase 4 — Autopilot (conditional)
- Require extensive paper and live execution validation, position-size limits, kill switch, settled-cash/buying-power checks, broker error handling and incident recovery.
- Validate behavior during volatility, outages, halts, partial fills and API failures.
- Obtain qualified legal/compliance advice before offering automated execution or paid trade recommendations to others.
- Release gradually with explicit risk acknowledgments and support procedures.

## 7. Sales and marketing approach

**Message:** “Watch every decision. Understand every result. Decide whether the tools fit your trading process.”

- **Top of funnel:** Academy tutorials, trade recaps, search-friendly explanations and original short clips.
- **Middle of funnel:** Evidence pages, annotated case studies, scanner walkthroughs and transparent limitations.
- **Conversion:** Waitlist, product demonstrations, limited beta, straightforward pricing and cancellation.
- **Retention:** Reliable scans, helpful alerts, actionable research, weekly release notes and responsive support.
- **Referrals:** Invite users to share the educational content and product experience, not performance promises.

Avoid aggressive “make money with our bot” advertising, cherry-picked winning trades, guaranteed returns, misleading backtests and undisclosed testimonials.

## 8. Operational and compliance gates

Before commercial launch, review with qualified counsel and relevant vendors:

- Investment-adviser, broker-dealer, signal-provider and auto-trading obligations, depending on product functionality and jurisdiction.
- Marketing and performance-presentation rules; hypothetical results, testimonials, endorsements and disclosures.
- Exchange/market-data licenses, including OPRA and any redistribution of live or historical quotes/charts.
- Broker API permissions, customer authorization, custody/security boundaries and data privacy.
- YouTube monetization, financial-content and reused/repetitive-content policies as applicable at launch.
- Business insurance, payment processing, taxes, customer agreements, incident handling and security controls.

Do not imply that “education only” language automatically removes regulatory obligations.

## 9. Scorecard and decision gates

| Area | Early KPI | Evidence needed before scaling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trading research | Reconciled trades, net expectancy, drawdown, uncertainty | Prospective results and robustness across market conditions |
| Scanner | Qualified setups, false positives, latency | Repeat use and meaningful time saved |
| YouTube | Returning viewers, retention, watch time | Sustainable production and audience growth |
| Website | Opt-in rate and qualified leads | Visitors returning for evidence/tools |
| Subscriptions | Activation, paid conversion, churn, gross margin | Repeatable acquisition with positive unit economics |
| Automation | Fill quality, errors, incidents, kill-switch tests | Operational readiness and legal clearance |

**Initial combined milestone:** approximately **100 verified trades per experiment plus 30 original YouTube videos**, followed by a structured review. These are learning milestones, not automatic launch approval.
