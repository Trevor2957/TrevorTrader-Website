Why it traded

A hypothetical reversal setup confirms before the illustrative entry. The candle spacing is a storytelling example, not a reconstruction of the deployed signal calculation.

What the option did

Four contracts enter at $0.24 and exit at $0.29: +$0.05 × 100 shares × 4 contracts = +$20 gross. Gross return on the $96 premium is 20.8%.

What we learned

The first target touch exits the example at 7:27. The later $0.38 peak is post-exit context, not profit earned or proof that holding longer is better.

Illustrative sizing and rounding: a $100 premium budget buys four $24 contracts, committing $96. That is a position budget, not $100 of stop-loss risk. This example rounds $0.24 × 1.17 up to $0.29 and $0.24 × 0.80 down to $0.19; the nominal 17/20 profile therefore becomes +20.8% / −20.8% at those prices. These cent-rounding and sizing assumptions are not verified production settings. A stop fill at $0.19 would lose $20 gross; gaps, slippage, and fees can increase the loss.

Why it traded

The invented stock breaks the prior $3.12 level with rising volume. Low float, news, liquidity, and all remaining gates are assumed; the picture alone does not prove them.

What the stock did

634 shares × ($3.35 − $3.15) = $126.80 gross. A fill at the $3.05 stop would instead lose $63.40 gross.

What we learned

This illustrates mechanics, not a profitable strategy. Fast, thin small caps can produce worse live fills than paper fills, and limit orders may remain unfilled.

Fill assumptions: the buy fills at the quoted ask and the target sell fills at the bid, with sufficient size for 634 shares. Fees and slippage are excluded. The example uses a $3.05 software stop; an actual structure-based stop may be higher. A software stop requires the local runner and connection to remain available. ABCD, its candles, and its fills are fictional.

Research status. CORE 17/20, RANGE 25/20, and SURGE $2K Challenge form the three paper experiments. CORE 30/20 is retired. Entry Integrity candidates remain separate from frozen controls. None of these illustrations establishes a validated edge.
